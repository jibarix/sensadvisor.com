Economic activity Economic Activity Index (IAE) · monthly

The Economic Activity Index combines payroll jobs, electricity generation, cement sales and gasoline consumption into one monthly reading of the island's economy. The dashed line is its 12-month average, which smooths out single-month noise.

Prices Puerto Rico CPI · change from a year earlier, %

Inflation for all items and four of the main spending groups, from the consumer price index the Puerto Rico Department of Labor publishes. Above zero, prices are higher than a year before.

Electricity-corrected line (estimate). The official index has held its electricity component at one value for years, so it misses every change in power rates. The green line in the first chart swaps that frozen value for the residential price the US Energy Information Administration measures each month (Electric Power Monthly, table 8.4). The price is converted to index points with the 2016–2018 ratio between the two, when both still moved, and weighted at 2.8% of all items, a share inferred from the index's own history because it is not published. It starts in January 2025, the first month with a year-earlier EIA price, and it can come in below the official rate when electricity gets cheaper. It is our estimate, not an official figure.

Consumer spending New vehicle sales and retail sales after inflation

New cars and light trucks sold each month (left axis, with the 12-month average dashed) and total retail sales in today's dollars (right axis, $ millions). Retail reporting restarted on a new basis in July 2025 that runs higher than the old one, so the two are drawn as separate lines and not compared across the break.

Tourism Airport passengers and hotel occupancy

Passengers through Luis Muñoz Marín airport each month (left axis, millions) and the occupancy rate of hotels and paradores (right axis, %). Cruise ship calls are in the indicator table above.

Construction Cement sales · thousand 94-pound bags a month

Cement sold each month (left axis, thousands of 94-pound bags, 12-month average dashed) is a quick read on building activity.

Government revenue Last 12 months, after inflation · $ millions

General Fund net revenues and the sales and use tax (IVU, 11.5%) added up over the trailing 12 months, so each point is a full year of collections and the seasonal swings cancel out.

External trade Last 12 months, after inflation · $ billions

Exports, imports and the difference between them, each summed over the trailing 12 months. Puerto Rico's exports are dominated by pharmaceuticals, so swings in a few plants move the total.

Electricity Generation and consumption · million kWh a month

Power generated and power sold to customers each month, with generation's 12-month average dashed. For the grid today, see PR Generation Status.

How to read it Method and known breaks

One month per indicator. Agencies publish on different schedules, so each indicator carries its own month. Series behind the freshest month are marked as lagging rather than shown as current.

Year-over-year first. Most series are not seasonally adjusted, so each is compared with the same month a year earlier. Rates, such as hotel occupancy, change in percentage points.

Corrected inflation. The bar and line marked “electricity corrected” are our estimate, explained under Prices. Dollar series below are still deflated with the official index.

After inflation. Retail sales, government revenue and trade are deflated with Puerto Rico's all-items consumer price index. That index publishes later than some dollar series, so a real change can trail the nominal one by a month or two.

Data sources: Puerto Rico Planning Board, Indicadores Económicos monthly workbook (Economic Activity Index, tourism, construction, revenues, trade, energy, bankruptcies, gasoline prices); PR Department of Labor (DTRH), consumer price index; DDEC, new vehicle sales and retail sales.
