01 / Principles

Sixsides

People, processes, technology, data, analytics, and governance have to move together.

FPeople

People know where the work bends and breaks. Their judgment belongs in the design.

RProcesses

Automation works best after the process makes sense. A faster bad process is still a bad process.

BTechnology

Systems work better when they connect cleanly. Data should move without constant repair.

UData

Good decisions need data people can find, understand, and trust. Clear definitions matter as much as clean records.

DAnalytics

Analytics should make a decision clearer. The method should fit the question, and the result should be explainable.

LGovernance

Clear rules make responsible use easier. People should know who decides, who owns, and who checks.

Process[SA.2.1]

A path to AI that works.

Some AI can start on day one. AI trusted with decisions and sensitive work follows a path.

  1. ListenStart with the people who do the work. Learn how it really runs, not how it’s documented.
  2. MapFollow real work from start to finish: every handoff, re-typed number and exception.
  3. DiagnoseFor each manual step: can the system not do it, or has nobody turned it on?
  4. Connect & centralizeSystems pass data to each other, and there’s one place where the numbers agree.
  5. PrioritizeSize the value of each opportunity and give it an owner. Quick wins first.
  6. Apply AIPut AI to work where the data is ready: reading documents, sorting requests, forecasting, answering questions.
  7. Keep it workingHuman review, a fallback, and a measure that shows it’s paying off.

03 / About

Jorge A. Arroyo

With over 10 years immersed in finance and analytics, I saw a critical need among local businesses: the need to bridge the gap between complex financial planning and the wealth of operational data often locked away in separate systems. SENS ADVISOR was born from an idea to address this: to empower organizations by integrating these domains through expert Extended Planning & Analysis (xP&A) and full-stack enterprise application development that transforms how businesses manage their financial and operational workflows.

I mainly focus on designing and deploying cloud-based xP&A and Business Intelligence systems using Microsoft Azure, Power BI, and advanced Excel techniques, while also architecting sophisticated full-stack business applications with enterprise-grade security, role-based access control, and real-time data synchronization.

Since 2022, I've pursued AI and LLM research as a purposeful hobby to stay current with rapidly evolving technology and to rigorously test what works versus what doesn't in real business implementations. This is how I maintain a ground-truth understanding of AI's current capabilities and limitations which allows me to provide clients with realistic assessments of AI opportunities, honest guidance on implementation risks, and evidence-based recommendations for efficiency gains.

What the symbols mean

The cube starts scrambled and solves itself as you scroll. These symbols describe its position and its moves.

g ∈ 𝒢, |𝒢| = 43,252,003,274,489,856,000
A cube has about 43 quintillion possible positions. 𝒢 means all of them; g is this page’s starting scramble.
𝒰 𝒟 ℛ ℒ ℱ ℬ
The six faces: Up, Down, Right, Left, Front and Back. A letter on its own means: turn that face a quarter turn clockwise, as if you were looking straight at it.
ℛ2 ℛ−1
2 means a half turn, counted as one move. −1 means a quarter turn the other way (counter-clockwise).
g = …
The scramble: the 20 random moves that mixed up this cube.
g−1 = …
The solve: the same 20 moves undone, last one first, each turned back the other way. A half turn undoes itself, so it stays the same.
Turn 01/20
The move playing now, which is also highlighted. At the end it shows 20/20 and e.
Section headers
The moves that play while you read that section.
g · g−1 = e
Scramble, then undo it, and the cube is solved again. e means “solved”.
↻
Picks a new random scramble. Your place on the page stays the same.

Any cube position can be solved in 20 moves or fewer, counting a half turn as one move. This page simply undoes its scramble, so its solve usually isn’t the shortest.
