Position within operating range 0% = Control line (lowest published) · 100% = Desborde crest (spillover)

Level source: USGS Threshold source: PRASA Where each measured level sits between two of PRASA's official published lines.

Each bar shows where a reservoir's measured level sits between its Control floor (PRASA's lowest published operating line, 0%) and its Desborde crest (the spillway, 100%). This is a position within the operating range, not percent of total water volume — a reservoir at 62% here is 62% of the way from its Control line up to the spillway, not holding 62% of its water. Bars are colored by the alert band the reservoir is currently in; reservoirs in the lowest operating bands sort to the top.

Historical · 2015 From the last published bathymetric survey. No newer survey is included here, so present-day losses may be larger.

Each bar is normalized to a reservoir's original design storage (100%): the solid blue part is the share still usable as of the 2015 survey, the faded brown part had silted in by then. Bars are ordered worst-first, so you can compare proportional loss regardless of reservoir size — Puerto Rico's older dams have lost heavily, several down to a third or less of their built capacity, reducing the water-storage buffer available during dry periods. Hover for the absolute volumes (Mm³). Source: DRNA/PRASA Plan Integral de Recursos de Agua (2016), Tabla 5.5 — a 2015 snapshot. Present-day siltation may be greater, but no newer survey is included here.
